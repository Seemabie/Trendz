Est. with care
— how trendz began

A tailoring shop, a grandmother, and a hundred-year-old box of thread.

Trendz Closet started as most family businesses do, with a grandmother who refused to let craft die. Her tailoring shop in Lahore dressed three generations of brides before her granddaughter, the woman who runs Trendz today, decided to bring it to New York.

We opened our Queens atelier in 2019. The grandmother is still in Lahore, still walking the workshop floor, still scolding the karigars when a single stitch is out of place. The thread she uses for our gold zardozi was milled by the same family for over fifty years.

This is not a brand built in a marketing meeting. It is a family kitchen with a different roof.

No. 02 · What We Believe

Three things we do not compromise on

i.

Hand, not machine.

Every motif is set by a human being. We do not use embroidery machines, ever. If a piece takes longer because of that, so be it. The difference is visible to anyone who looks closely.

ii.

Honest prices.

We quote you once. The number we say is the number you pay. No surprise alteration fees, no inflated rush charges, no markup for being a bride. Our pricing is the same for everyone.

iii.

Fair wages.

Our karigars are paid above market, year round, with a small share of every commission. When you commission a Trendz piece, you are supporting fifteen families in Lahore. We are proud of that.

No. 03 · A Timeline

The milestones

Six years of building, slowly and on purpose.

2019

The first storefront

We open a small studio on 118th Avenue in Jamaica, Queens. Six dresses on the rack, one sewing machine, one part-time tailor. The first bride walks in within forty-eight hours.

2020

The pandemic year

We close the storefront for three months and pivot to video consultations. Brides postpone weddings but not their dresses. We deliver eighteen bridal gowns by FedEx that year, every fitting by Zoom.

2021

The Lahore workshop

We bring on a dedicated workshop of nine karigars in Lahore. No more freelance hopping between embroiderers. One team, one standard, year-round salaries.

2022

The bridal atelier

We expand the Queens space and dedicate one room to bridal consultations only. Chai, sketchbooks, silk swatches in a hundred shades. Bridal becomes half our business.

2024

First five hundred brides

We dress our five hundredth bride. We send each one a hand-pressed silk thread bookmark on their first anniversary. Many come back for their sister's wedding.

2026

Today

Fifteen karigars in Lahore. Five seamstresses in Queens. One head designer who still answers every bridal WhatsApp herself. And you, reading this.
